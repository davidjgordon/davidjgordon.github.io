TEACHING & MENTORSHIP / DAVID J. GORDON

Politics across scales and boundaries.

My teaching brings together international relations, political theory, global environmental politics, and urban studies. I encourage students to connect theoretical questions about authority, representation, and collective action to contemporary issues of climate change and global governance.

Courses at UC Santa Cruz

POLI 70

Global Politics

Introduces major actors, institutions, and debates in global politics, with attention to the political dynamics of cooperation, conflict, and global change.

POLI 160A

Theories of International and World Politics

Engages major theoretical approaches to world politics and asks how their assumptions shape explanations of global power, authority, order, and political change.

POLI 174

Global Political Ecology

Examines the politics of environmental change, with attention to power, inequality, the institutions of governance, and competing visions of sustainability.

POLI 190F

Cities in World Politics

Explores cities as sites and participants in international politics, including city diplomacy, transnational networks, urban climate action, and the politics of global urban governance.

POLI 213

Who Governs the Globe? Shifting Authority in Contemporary World Politics

Graduate-level inquiry into authority beyond the nation-state, examining international institutions, transnational actors, and the changing organization of global governance.

Graduate mentorship

I supervise and serve on graduate research committees engaging international relations, comparative politics, global environmental politics, climate governance, and the politics of cities. I am especially interested in projects investigating political authority across governance scales, transnational networks, the institutional politics of sustainability transitions, and urban climate governance in comparative perspective.
